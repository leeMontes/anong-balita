Getting Started

Anong Balita? is a pocket walkie-talkie and chat that runs right in the browser. Spin up a channel, hand out the handle or QR, and everyone who tunes in can talk push-to-talk and text each other.

Start a Channel

1Choose Create on the Radio tab.
2Name your Balita Group and pick a reusable Channel Handle (3–32 characters).
3Tap Broadcast. You become the base station.
4Share the handle or let people scan the QR.

Tune In

1Choose Join, enter the handle and a name.
2Tap Tune In — or scan the QR to fill it in.
The handle is remembered, so you can reuse it any time.

Push to Talk & Chat

Hold the red Push to Talk button (or the spacebar) to transmit. Release to listen — a roger beep confirms you're off the air.
The OPS counter shows how many units are connected.
Use the Chat tab to send text to everyone on the channel.

Background Audio

The app asks the browser to keep the radio alive so you keep receiving when the tab is hidden or the phone is idle.
On iOS Safari, Apple suspends background web audio, so receiving may pause when the app is fully minimized — a platform limit, not a bug.

Where Is My Data Saved?

Short answer: it isn't. There is no database and no recording.
Voice  travels peer-to-peer over encrypted WebRTC, mixed live on the base station. Nothing is recorded.
Chat  is relayed live through the base station and kept only in memory — cleared when you end the channel.
Signaling  uses the public PeerJS cloud only to introduce browsers. No audio or messages are stored there.
QR codes  are drawn locally in your browser.
Group name, handle and code  stay in memory. Your last handle is kept on your own device only, so you can resume.
No accounts, no cookies, no analytics, no tracking.

Notes & Limits

The base station must stay connected; if the host leaves, the channel closes.
The more units connected, the more the base station carries.
Microphone access is required — grant it when asked.
Everyone on the same handle hears and sees each other. Don't share a handle you don't trust.
